PROMOTION MEMO: loan-default (2026-11-25)

Decision: promote version 1, the balanced logistic regression (run logreg-C=1.0-class_weight=balanced), to @champion and keep version 2, the balanced random forest (run forest-max_depth=8-class_weight=balanced), as @challenger. Recall is the deciding metric because a missed defaulter costs the lender the loan principal, while a wrongly flagged good borrower only costs a manual recheck. On the 1,000-row held-out test set (222 real defaults) version 1 has recall 0.865, precision 0.611 and AUC 0.923; version 2 has recall 0.788, precision 0.742 and AUC 0.925. Version 1 therefore catches 192 of the 222 defaults and misses 30, while version 2 catches 175 and misses 47, so the champion lets 17 fewer defaulters through per 1,000 applications. The price is lower precision (0.611 against 0.742): version 1 flags 314 applications instead of 236, which means about 78 more manual reviews per 1,000 applications, an acceptable cost next to 17 extra defaults. AUC is effectively a tie (0.923 against 0.925, a gap of 0.002), so it does not change the decision; it only confirms that both models rank borrowers equally well and that the difference lies in how many defaults are caught at the 0.5 cut-off. Among the two logistic regressions with the same top recall (C=1.0 and C=0.1, both 0.865), C=1.0 was chosen on the precision tie-break (0.611 against 0.610). Version 2 stays registered as @challenger because it is the strongest contrasting model (highest AUC of the six runs and clearly higher precision) and is the natural candidate if the review workload ever becomes the binding constraint.

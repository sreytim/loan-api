Justification (read from charts 6a-psi-income.png and 6b-risk.png):
- Warning at 0.1: for the first five days income PSI stays between 0.034 and 0.073, so 0.1 sits just above the normal band and is first crossed on day 6 (0.172). It is cheap to raise and only asks for attention.
- Alert at 0.2 on two consecutive days: income falls by the same 8 USD every day, yet the measured PSI does not rise smoothly. It falls from day 6 to day 7 (0.172 to 0.132, -0.040) and from day 10 to day 11 (0.326 to 0.236, -0.090). Those reversals are sampling noise, because each day is only 400 applications split over 10 bins (about 40 per bin). The first day above 0.2 is day 8 at 0.236, only 0.036 above the line, which is smaller than the noise just measured (0.040 to 0.090), so one reading cannot be trusted. Day 9 (0.240) confirms it, and the alert is raised on day 9. The rule costs one day of delay and removes pages caused by a single noisy sample.
- Mean predicted default above 0.416: the day-1 baseline is 0.366, and while income PSI is still under 0.1 (days 1 to 3) the value moves only between 0.355 and 0.369 (spread 0.014). A margin of 0.05 is about three and a half times that spread, so normal days stay below it; it is first crossed on day 7 (0.434) and never comes back under. The threshold is set relative to the model's own baseline, not to the actual default rate, because the champion was trained with class_weight=balanced and its probabilities sit above the observed rate from day 1 (0.366 predicted against 0.255 actual, gap +0.111).
- The actual default rate rises too (0.255 on day 1 to 0.367 on day 14), so the drift is real risk and not only a statistical artefact. In production the labels arrive months later, which is why the two input-side signals above are the alerts and the actual rate is only a later confirmation.
